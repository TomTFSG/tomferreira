Carrie (2026-) by episode

Here are my notes for each episode of Carrie (2026-). These are loose thoughts put on to keyboard as I watched each episode so take them with a grain of salt. For my thoughts on this and all other Carrie adaptations go here.

Episode 1:

The isolation of Carrie, her moms paranoia, it's very believable for what a modern day american hermit cult would be. I am glad they introduced Carrie's misfitness early on and putting her in school for the first time ever in the outside world emphasizing that awkwardness and making it feel plausible instead of ridiculous. She is the new girl and has no prior relation to any of the other kids so they can't justify her bullying as in "she has always been weird before" and forcing the show to tell us exactly what made her a target for bullying, something I missed in other adaptations. Chris so far is handled very well, giving her more layers with her parents divorce will probably work out better in favor of a better story. I don't like that last line referring to some “others”. I don't want more than one supernatural teenager in this story. One is enough. Fuck off with that multiple season bullshit. Everything else is a near perfect King adaptation in the modern world. For a moment I thought this would be the pseudo-documentary I dreamed this could be with witness interviews and what not, but it seems like it is just a way to foreshadow future events which isn’t a total disappointment and works well enough. I do not like the “it wasn’t just Carrie” comment. That’s not the story I want told.

Episode 2:

I think I teared up at the scene in the library. The music choices for the first episode felt weird at times, it fits the tone and all but in some scenes it kind of feels distracting. The score for that library scene is beautiful. The little clairvoyant scene in the bathroom works as a visual replacement for those gut feelings described in the book. Poor baby Carrie, I'm so sorry for what is about to happen to you. I swear to God if there's a Stranger Things like army Fighting Carrie in the finale I'm gonna be so mad. I like how they keep teasing the ending and slowly building it up. Like with Billy appearing before he met Chris. The modernization feels right and realistic. If King were to write the book today I think this would be a good way to imagine it. I keep liking Chris and how she feels. Both she and Sue (for now) feel like realistic teenage girls. Even the slang isn't too much, just enough through text or in random conversations to feel real but not too "life is strangey" (idk how else to describe it). I'm curious as to how Brookly will fit into this. I didn't love the vídeo posting in the 2013 adaptation, I still don't adore it in this one but at least it feels more realistic and gives more context for why Chris would do it (to rebel against the idea of there being people who are "off-limits" to her). If they project that shit during prom again, that will probably be it for me. Also Carrie using her powers at will for the first time is great. Please keep slowly building up her learning to use her powers.

Episode 3:

I like the logo slowly turning more and more red. The V.O narration feels familiar, as if taking the role the narrator had in the book, providing occasional context to this or that thing as the story required it. Connecting Carrie with old witch hunts feels like a natural progression to take. Emaline is an interesting addition to the cast. I'm almost sure Margaret has the shine too, and it would make sense given the genetic context given in the book). I desperately want Carrie to be happy. That was the appeal to me in the book and it is again in this. Most adaptations show this by some sort of crush on Tommy, which is the fastest way to do it if you're limited to 90 minutes plus change to tell the story, as a TV show it has the freedom to take more time and explore Carrie's desire to fit in with parties and friends and what not. She probably shouldn't drink or at least not get blackout drunk, I think she should keep some sense of modesty and still be avoidant of those things as that modesty and conservative way to see the world are part of her character. The music choice is odd at times but at others it's absolutely perfect. I like them giving more depth to Billy. This is starting to feel less like an adaptation and more of a retelling. Like how Snow White by Disney is a retelling of a fairytale while adding its own flair to it. It isn't going against the lore, but it adds so many new things it isn't exactly the same. The little bit of Carrie watching the stars with Tommy is great, and the parallel between her and Chris works very well too. I'm not loving the emphasis on the powers so I'm scared of what Emaline means in all this.

Episode 4:

I don't love the line about "men taking down women". I respect the idea of trying to make it a feminist issue, but it's not the theme the rest of the show seems to be going for so it feels odd here. Making posts and online discourse a bigger part of the plot actually seems to be working. It doesn't feel plugged in at the last second like in the 2013 adaptation and it actually makes for good drama. It modernizes the story and goes along with its themes. Kids are cruel and hurt others to lash out. The whole thing can still backfire on them so I'm holding my judgement until the end. I'm still confused about Emaline. Who is she? Why is she here? Margaret's stalker-like cameras work well for her over protective mother personality.Watching her at work builds the rift between her and the rest of society. Makes her seem more human and less the villain she was in the book, it humanizes her making her more of a person than the somewhat simple villainous person she is in the book that probably wouldn't work as well if she was adapted directly. Some scenes don't need music. The music in the talk between Carrie and Sue is distracting, an instrumental track would be better. They're really working to make Chris more relatable, which is something I think other adaptations really miss. The '76 adaptation was pretty good, and faithful to the book. This one works really well for a version of the story where she and Sue are closer and humanizes her way more than the 02 or the 13 adaptation and I love that honestly. Billy is perfect. He's not the raging sociopath the latest adaptations made him out to be, he is evil, sure, disturbed and a criminal. But you can see how that would be attractive to ágil like Chris. How Chris fell in love with the lunatic Billy was in other adaptations I can only understand if she herself is a nutcase and when she is I immediately lose interest in the story. His little speech about being a hero for not dropping a brick on people is deluded and stupid. But so is he so it tracks.The lighting in this bit is very dark and hard to see. I don't love it. Margaret being a vengeful bitch is crazy work but I kind of love it. I love how horrible she is. Ralph's ghost haunts the narrative more than he did in the book. I like this version of the story a lot. High as a kite Carrie on drugs she doesn't know she's on is kinda funny. All night scenes are dark as hell and I can't see anything. The blowout between Sue and Chris is great, Chris is a bad person but deep down she's alone and that's the problem with lonely people, they find comfort in the wrong places. There's a lot of Carrie walking the streets alone this episode, the foreshadowing is great. I'm starting to think the show doesn't have enough time to wrap up all the threads. Or at least, it won't take enough time setting up the actual prom as I want it to.

Episode 5:

Do these little monologues that open the episodes actually lead anywhere? Is this feminism thread actually going somewhere? Or is it just setting up an eventual season 2? I don't love the idea of that, I hope I get answers soon. I really wanted Matthew Lillard to tell Chris's dad to shove it and all. It's one of my favorite bits in the book, the principal putting this dad in his place. It can still happen, I'm holding on hope. Emaline sometimes feels like someone's OC pushed in the narrative for some reason. I would've preferred Carrie figuring her powers on her own. I don't know how much of Ralph they adapted directly from the book, but if they adapted what they did, that would explain why Margaret is so against picking up his remains. Having Carrie hurt others with her power before the clímax… I don't know how I feel about that. It works for this I guess but I'm not 100% on board with it. Is my favorite sub genre, teenage girls with supernatural powers? Between this and Life is Strange I'm sensing a pattern. Someone definitely wrote Emaline while thinking of Chloe. This is so Max and Chloe coded they just need to start making out. This is half Chris's show. I like it a lot. This show gets social media in a very modern way. Margaret is way more humane than she is in the book. I am curious as to how they will adapt her in the climax. I did realize that Emaline is just Carrie's imaginary friend or someone she is seeing using the shining, either way it is clever. I confess I didn't love Emaline at first but now I get why Chris thought she was weird at the party, she saw Carrie talking to herself. I don't adore this version of the adaptation, I don't love Carrie using her powers at all against others before the climax. I don't love that Chris has access to the footage Margaret recorded of Carrie confessing to having powers. I was okay with the cameras if they were to be used as evidence of Margaret controlling her daughter to eventually make Carrie snap. The Direction is good for the most part, scenes at night are too dark but apart from that I have no issue but there are no super complex shots that blew me away. Sue taking action is very true to her character. I once again fear that prom night doesn't have enough build up.

Episode 6:

You know its gonna be brutal af when they hit you with that suicide and self-harm warning. I want it to be brutal. Make it brutal. The redder logo is great. Do these scenes at the start mean anything? I kind of want references to other King stories (the shining and It or other novels with characters that shine). I'm curious to see where this is going. So, the cameras were used like I wanted them to. Good,good. I confess I thought this was only six episodes long thus my worry about possible loose ends. With two more episodes I think it'll be alright. I'm relieved.(Cartman voice) "How do I reach these kiiiids?" That other teacher is gonna turn out to be a dick isn't he? So Carrie is now Danny Torance, I kind of like it. 1655 posts? 522 victims? How long has it been? How much time does she spend on that phone? Yes, principal Lillard let him have it. God, he's such a good actor. The fnaf movies could've been so great… Chris's parents being the angel and devil on her shoulder is such a great little bit. Why did she call it a pic? It was a vídeo before? Tommy's a good guy, I like him. Bold, a suicidal teacher. Very bold. My god that just raises the stakes by a lot. Holy shit this is so modern américa it's crazy. I loved that twist, holy shit. In another universe the girl playing Chirs could've been Carrie. With good makeup, posture and acting (which I think she could pull off) she would do great. But the actress they do have portraying the titular character is also very good. I feel for Carrie and she looks mostly like I pictured her in my head. Not ugly, but not a super-model either. I want her to look beautiful in that prom dress. "There is no mental health, just weakness of spirit". This is some top notch commentary on the state of modern teenage life and teenage mental health. All aspects of the story are slowed down and that works in the show's favor. It's a great retelling of the story. I love the confrontation in the bathroom between Chris and Carrie. I love how it's directed, how it is written, and how it's acted. I love how the kids look like kids. Casting did a very good job to not make them all Riverdale buff, hot and sexy twenty somethings. Carrie's final plea for an apology feels powerful. I liked that her voice cracked a little. And Margaret just made the whole thing worse for shits and giggles. "That's three words" you go Carrie. The escalation of everything feels so American. Jesus that's terrifying. I think this might have gone too far the other direction. There is too much history between Carrie and Chris' at this point. The parents are now involved in a way that feels too large and too complicated. Also a vast majority of the student body seems to be on Carrie's side, so it changes the perception of having the mob at prom be Carrie's misunderstood bullies, this single minded mob-mentality of a crowd how judge and misunderstand Carrie is suddenly gone and replaced for a group of violent teens who see her as a de facto leader(or at least a symbol) of a movement against Chris, their collective enemy. Chris's action of dropping the bucket of blood on Carrie in the book could be seen as an act of the whole student body since some were complacent to her bullying, or aware of it without doing anything. Right now, everyone antagonizes her so that wrinkle is gone. I also resent the break-up between Tommy and Sue, I liked that they were together even when he took Carrie to prom. Like a thematic peace offering in the shape of a date. Carrie is kind, she's empathetic, she's beautiful. And I love her final confrontation with Margaret. Absolutely amazing. Give Summer Howell and Samantha Sloyan all the flowers.

Episode 7:

I’m curious to see where this is going now. There are 2 hours to lead up to prom and the prom itself. I'm glad Amanda from the cold open actually became a character. I still know nothing about the narrator though. I feel like there was some rush to the build up to the prom. Tommy is taking Carrie, out of his own volition instead as a favour to Sue eliminating the ambiguity from investigators as to whether or not Sue was involved in bathing Carrie in blood. Carrie and Sue didn’t even talk about it. He’s her ex, wouldn’t Sue be a little mad or caught off guard? Even if she and Carrie are friends? The Billy and Chris bit is also cut short. They just showed up with a bucket of blood on the day like idiots. I liked the pre-fire prom in the book, when Carrie feels involved and accepted for the first time in the book. In this that would feel pointless but I still miss it. Who chose this playlist? Carrie and Tommy do feel like friends, I preferred it when Tommy started falling for Carrie but for this version of the story, this works. I wanted more drama in the calling of the names. More emphasis, more… fear. In the end the actual prom itself feels like an after thought, a thing you need to get through because you can’t just skip to fire and blaze and death. It was rushed and it undermines the most iconic event in the story. The cut to black was perfect I do have to admit. There was no other way to finish that episode than having Carrie open her eyes and the screams that follow as we stare at a black screen.

Episode 8:

I don’t want a season 2. I don't care about these other people with TK. Just give me a good Carrie. Pushing the narrative back to episode 1 works well enough. I like Sue and the actress well enough, she sells the whole regret thing. Black prom in all its glory starts now. I don’t care for the Emaline persona of it all, I am choosing to ignore it for a better viewing experience. But I can't, they keep bringing her up. She isn’t actually Carrie’s imaginary friend and instead an actual person astral projecting visions to her. I didn't like that choice. I don't like these other strangers here to set up a second season. I very much didn’t like that. Nor the Carrie White commission/memory erasing party. Everything else was amazing. The black prom, the chase for Billy and Chris was great. The action is cool but whenever I feel like I am finally getting immersed again they bring back Emaline. They quite literally took Carrie’s greatest moment of agency where she’s had enough and gave the agency to some girl we don’t understand the motivations of. The action itself was great, a perfect adaptation of how Carrie acted and of what she did in the book. My personal preferences would have had an entranced Carrie destroying more of the town, all adaptations limit it to the gym or school while in the book she blows up the gas main, floods streets and takes down electrical poles as she moves creating a path of destruction behind her. The choice to ignore most of the religious fanatical part of Margaret and Carrie's backstory would have made the church visit pointless, but I still miss it. I expected more and less at the same time. I very much loved the final moments of Chris. She attempting to steer the car away last minute before Carrie flipping it over, the pointless last second attempt at an apology, it was nearly perfect (once again Emaline ruined it) It’s its own version of the story but it seems that it got distracted by a potential season 2 half way through introducing elements that affected the story in the wrong direction (Emaline and the Others). If those two elements were to be removed this could have been a near perfect retelling of King’s original story. The insistence on having this multi-season story prevents this from being the story I wanted it to be. And I am not sure I want another Stranger Things like story about telekinetic children. That wasn’t why I signed up for this show. The tragedy of Carrie ends in her death. Keeping her alive undermines the story’s theme. I don’t hate this version, in many aspects I like it a lot. But it fails to tell a satisfying, even if tragic, story. Because of its insistence on setting up a sequel. I don’t know if I’ll return for Carrie season 2, I’ll decide whenever the trailer drops inevitably but I very much doubt it. I didn’t like that that woman was the one to stop Margaret’s heart or the absolute audacity to call her power the Shine. I don’t care for her as a villain or a hero or whatever she is. I don’t know who she is or her pawns. The more I think about it, the more I hate it and the more frustrated I feel about it. It lost me at the very end, a shame really.
